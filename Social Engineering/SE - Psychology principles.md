# SE - Psychology principles

**TL;DR:** SE works by triggering deep-rooted human instincts that normally help us but backfire online. Knowing the list lets you both craft a pretext and recognise one being used on you/a client.

from: SE - Social Engineering course doc

## The levers

- **Trust** — impersonate a colleague/manager/helpdesk; group identity ("I'm from your team") lowers guard.
- **Authority** — orders from "the boss" / IT. Basis of CEO fraud and business email compromise (BEC).
- **Curiosity** — sensational links, dropped USB sticks people plug in.
- **Helpfulness** — "Can you open this for me, I can't access it?" People want to assist.
- **Fear / urgency (fight-or-flight)** — "Your account locks in 30 min", unpaid fine. Stress kills critical thinking → faster clicks.
- **Reciprocity** — do a small favour / give a freebie first, then ask for something back.
- **Social proof** — "Your colleagues already completed this", fake reviews/likes.
- **Automatism** — mimic routine comms ("here's the monthly report") so it's actioned on autopilot.
- **Scarcity** — "Last chance", "only 1 left" → hasty decisions.
- **Laziness** — "Click here, it's faster"; abuse of saved passwords and auto-trust.
- **Overconfidence / optimism** — "I won't fall for this / that only happens to big companies" → ignored warnings.

Fear and greed are the two most commonly exploited. Most real attacks stack several levers (e.g. authority + urgency).

## Defensive takeaway

Verify out-of-band (call a known number, not the one provided), slow down under urgency, and use agreed code words for emergency money/access requests.
